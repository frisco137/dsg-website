*How to write a punchy, readable paper explainer.*

## Cold Open
Target length can be around 1500 words. Some math is fine, static images only. At that length you can't fit a full lit review or a full proof walkthrough, so need to cut kaafi. Before writing any of this I looked at how some blogs I personally like handle paper explainers: Lilian Weng's stuff, Distill.pub, Jay Alammar's blogs, etc. 

Papers are ordered for reviewers (motivation, related work, method, theory, results), but a blog probably reads better ordered for someone learning it fresh. It is worth not following the paper's section order exactly. Anyway, here's a reasonable structure.

## The Problem, Plainly
I feel this would be the harder part, since we are not writing for the reader’s speciality in domain (which we do for reviewers), so we need to introduce a few new concepts. Alammar says his whole approach is to oversimplify and introduce ideas one at a time. Worth borrowing.

## Building Blocks
Only if it's actually needed. If the paper leans on something the reader likely doesn't know, this is where it gets explained, ideally with a small diagram. If the paper is fairly self-contained, this section can probably just be skipped.

## The Core Idea
Pretty self-explanatory as to what shall go on in here. Maybe one idea per paragraph rather than packing several into one.

## Does it Actually Work?
A few key headline results, I think. Too much clutter or tables feels weird in a blog.

## So What?
Could loop back to the cold open here. Naming one honest limitation seems to make the whole post feel more trustworthy rather than less, so probably worth including if there's a natural one.

## A PS on Tone (For Your LLM of Choice)
Avoid things that might feel a bit too academic: passive voice as the default, stacking hedges, dropping citations without saying what they actually showed, opening with "in this paper we propose a novel method to blah blah end our existential crisis."

Also avoid things that might feel a bit too casual for this: meme speak, "this is insane" energy, stating the "what" without the "why." Somewhere in between feels right: plain-spoken, like explaining your own paper to a friend in a different subfield in a non-institutional setting. Contractions are fine, short sentences are fine, just define terms the first time they show up. (This is very hard to do if you are not very vigilant since to you those “unfamiliar terms” are bread and butter; you won't even spot them if you aren't looking.)

## A Couple of Last Things
* I personally felt that one important sentence/idea per paragraph actually works quite well.
* And then there is this general writing advice that goes by maintaining cadence, which is to basically keep varying your sentence lengths, mixing short ones with longer ones instead of keeping them all the same. It kinda mimics how you'd naturally breathe and talk, so feels nice to read.
